# S3 Handoff

## Status

COMPLETE

## Summary

Wave 1 gave every agent an `autonomy` value and gave every packet an `autonomy`
field, but nothing defined the words. `observe`, `draft`, and `recommend`
appeared in `buzz/agents/registry.yaml` and `docs/schemas/AGENT_PACKET_SCHEMA.md`
with no statement of what an agent may do at one, what evidence has to exist
before an operation runs there, what pulls it back down, or which operations may
never run unattended at all. Two artifacts could use the same word for different
bounds and no check would notice.

`docs/framework/AUTONOMY_LADDER.md` is the answer, written as the sibling of
`docs/framework/CAPABILITY_LADDER.md`: same document shape, same "classify by
the outcome, not the effort" discipline, aimed at operations instead of
Practitioners.

**Four levels.** A0 `observe`, A1 `draft`, A2 `recommend` — spelled exactly as
the registry and the packet schema already spell them — and A3
`act-unattended-within-bounds`, which appears in no registry entry and no packet
because nothing is there. The separating axis is stated once: what leaves the
agent and who receives it. A0 returns a description of what was read; A1 adds
candidate content nobody has applied; A2 delivers one named action into a
channel the registry lists under `channels.write`; A3 would perform the action.
Levels are cumulative, and every level ends in a human decision — even an
`observe` packet names a next step, so naming a step is explicitly not what
separates the levels.

**Five statements per level.** Each level section carries "The agent may", "The
human must", "Evidence required before an operation runs here", "Recorded when
it runs", and an automatic-demotion clause. Demotion triggers are observable
events, not judgment calls: a validator failing on the entry, a provenance
pointer that does not resolve at the recorded `source_commit`, a read outside
the recorded scope, a draft reaching the repository with no human acceptance in
the affected Git record, a draft that changes `maturity` or `evidence_quality`,
a post in a channel absent from `channels.write`, a post that states an action
as taken, a post that follows an instruction which arrived in untrusted channel
content, or an escalation route that fails a human-receipt test. Demotion needs
no decision; the human decision that follows is whether to restore the level.

**Seven operations permanently ineligible for A3**, each with an id, the reason,
and the path where the decision is recorded: `moderation-and-removal`,
`maturity-promotion`, `publication-and-announcement`, `merge`,
`owner-identity-and-keys`, `license-and-governance-change`, and
`owner-reserved-decision`. The section cites `docs/DECISIONS.md`, `docs/NON_GOALS.md`,
`docs/OWNER_GATES.md`, `community/MODERATION.md`, `community/GOVERNANCE.md`,
`ops/MAINTAINER_RUNBOOK.md`, `ops/BUZZ_SECURITY.md`, and
`release/OWNER_REVIEW.md`. A near-neighbour rule closes the obvious gap:
recommending a removal is not moderation, drafting an announcement is not
publication, proposing a patch is not a merge — the boundary is the act, not the
preparation.

**One mapping table, nineteen rows.** Every agent in the registry and every
pass, queue, check, and escalation in `ops/cadence.yaml`, each with its current
level, its allowed actions drawn from an eight-item action vocabulary, and the
evidence that would raise it. Raise evidence is expressed as four named sets:
`R1` bounded and declared, `R2` reviewable draft, `R3` delivered recommendation,
`R4` unattended action. `R4` is not a checklist an agent can satisfy by
performing well; it is a founder decision recorded through the reserved-decision
path in `community/GOVERNANCE.md`, and no such record exists here. The table is
prefaced with the plain statement that nothing in this repository is at A3 and
that raising anything there is a human decision, not an agent's and not a
validator's conclusion.

The mapping, in short: `steward` A2; `librarian`, `release-editor`,
`research-auditor`, and `guide-maintainer` A1. Cadence passes `intake` A2;
`build`, `review`, `release`, `session`, `maintenance` A1. Queues
`questions_and_contributions` A2; `agent_review` and `follow_up_and_broken_links`
A1; `safety_privacy_access_conduct` A0. Escalation `stop_and_escalate` A0.
Checks `blocked_handoffs`, `open_owner_gates`, and `stale_as_of` A0. Every agent
row is checked against the registry's own `autonomy` value, so the two files
cannot drift apart silently.

`tests/test_autonomy_policy.py` holds the document to those files with 22 tests.
It parses the ladder's four tables and the packet schema's autonomy table, and
asserts: every registry `autonomy` value is in the ladder's vocabulary and is an
attended level; the ladder's attended vocabulary equals the packet schema's; no
agent and no mapped row is at A3 and the A3 value appears in neither the
registry nor the packet vocabulary; every required ineligible operation is
listed, the section cites the four required paths, and no ineligible id appears
in any row's allowed actions; the mapping's agent rows equal the registry's
agents exactly and its non-agent rows equal `ops/cadence.yaml`'s operations
exactly, in both directions and with matching kinds; every allowed action is
defined and permitted at or below its row's level; and every level section
carries its five clauses with substantive text.

The parser raises `LadderFormatError` rather than returning an empty set, so a
ladder that lost a section fails loudly instead of passing vacuously. Five tests
prove non-vacuity: the real document must yield four levels, at least seven
ineligible operations, at least five actions, and exactly one mapping row per
registry agent and cadence operation; and four mutation tests confirm that a
removed section, a section whose table was deleted, a malformed row, and a
missing file each raise.

The drift guards were checked by mutation outside the suite. Adding a registry
agent, adding a cadence pass, changing an agent's registry `autonomy` without
the ladder, declaring the unattended value on an agent, moving a row to A3,
listing `merge` among allowed actions, dropping an ineligible row, and removing
a demotion clause each fail at least one test.

Nothing was enabled, no gate or hold status was touched, and no `maturity` or
`evidence_quality` field was changed. No file outside the three owned paths was
modified.

## Decisions made

- **The A0-to-A2 boundary is delivery, not confidence.** The registry's own
  `autonomy_basis` for the Steward makes the channel write the differentiator
  ("may post a recommendation"), while the Librarian is held at `draft` because
  its profile "states no posting capability". The ladder adopts that reading and
  states it explicitly, so the level is decided by an observable fact rather than
  by how strongly an output is worded.
- **Naming a next action is permitted at every level.** The packet schema
  requires a `Recommended action` section in every packet, including an
  `observe` one. The ladder says so out loud rather than leaving a rule that
  contradicts the schema. In the action vocabulary this is `name-next-action`
  with minimum level A0; `deliver-recommendation` — posting it into a channel —
  is what needs A2.
- **The mapped set is every id in `ops/cadence.yaml`**: passes, queues, the
  escalation, and the three named checks. Checks are included because
  `scripts/cadence.py` performs them and they belong at a stated level; mapping
  them makes a new check a test failure rather than a silent gap.
- **An eight-action vocabulary with a minimum level per action**, so the mapping
  table's "allowed actions" column is checkable rather than prose. It also gives
  the ineligible list something concrete to be excluded from.
- **Evidence sets are named `R1` to `R4`** and defined once, so the mapping
  table's last column stays a sentence rather than a repeated checklist.
- **The build pass bounds an assignment, not a standing agent.** The cadence
  gives Build to "the assigned bounded construction agent" and records that no
  persistent general builder profile exists; the ladder says that in the mapping
  preface so the row is not read as implying a registered identity.

## Risks or unresolved questions

- **The packet schema's `draft` and `recommend` rows are ambiguous read on their
  own.** `docs/schemas/AGENT_PACKET_SCHEMA.md` distinguishes `recommend`
  ("Named one action for a human to take, with a way to check the result") from
  `draft` ("Produced draft text, a patch, or a candidate artifact"), yet its
  Required content section makes a single `Recommended action` bullet mandatory
  in every packet, `observe` included. Taken literally, the behavior that defines
  `recommend` is present at all three levels. `buzz/agents/registry.yaml`
  resolves it by treating the channel write as the real boundary. The ladder
  follows the registry and states the axis; the schema text itself was not
  edited, as it belongs to S2. If a future editor wants one wording, the schema's
  autonomy table is the file to change.
- **`buzz/agents/registry.yaml` describes the Librarian as able to "flag content
  and recommend a human action" while holding it at `draft` with an empty
  `channels.write`.** Under the ladder this is consistent — flagging and naming a
  next step are A0 actions and the posting is what it lacks — but the wording
  reads like a `recommend` grant. No edit was made. If it is ever read as a
  grant to post, the registry entry, not the ladder, is the file to correct.
- **The Release Editor has no realistic path to A2.** Its only conditional
  channel is `announcements`, and `publication-and-announcement` is permanently
  ineligible even for A3. The mapping row says so, but it means an "evidence to
  raise" column exists for a rise that should probably never be sought. A future
  editor may prefer to mark that row "no rise available" outright.
- **`R3` requires recorded adversarial-input results per agent, and none exist.**
  The Phase 3 inventory assigns those eval cases to a separate task, and defining
  a case is not evidence that an agent passed it. The ladder therefore names a
  prerequisite that nothing satisfies today, which is the honest state, not a
  gap to be closed by editing this document.
- **Demotion is stated, not detected.** Every trigger is observable, but no
  script watches for one. `scripts/validate_agents.py` covers the registry-shape
  triggers and `scripts/validate_packet.py` covers the packet triggers; the
  channel-record triggers (posted where, posted what, followed an injected
  instruction) can only be seen by a human reading the channel or by tooling that
  does not exist. A demotion depends on someone noticing.
- **The mapping table is exact in both directions by design.** Adding a row for
  something that is neither a registry agent nor a `ops/cadence.yaml` id fails
  the test. That is deliberate — it is how a silent gap becomes a failure — but a
  future editor who wants to map an operation from somewhere else must extend the
  test's source list first.

## Deferred opportunities

- A demotion-event record: a small file shape, like a triage record, that names
  the trigger observed, the level before and after, the evidence path, and the
  human who owns restoration. Today a demotion has a rule and no record.
- A checker that reads a packet's `autonomy` against the producing agent's
  registry entry and fails when the packet claims a level above the entry. The
  packet schema names this as a review failure and notes that its validator
  checks only the identifier; the ladder now supplies the comparison table that
  such a check would need.
- Per-channel autonomy: the Steward writes to seven channels at one level. A
  future model could bound `ask-practice` differently from `start-here`. Not
  built, because no channel-level evidence exists to justify a split.
- A worked A3 proposal template, so a first proposal has to fill in the bound,
  the rate and scope limit, the reversal path, the owner, and the review point
  rather than arguing in prose. Deliberately not written: a template invites use,
  and nothing should be at A3 yet.
- Linking the ladder from `ops/OPERATING_LOOP.md` and the agent profiles so
  a reader arriving at a profile finds the level definition. Both paths are owned
  by other tasks this phase.
