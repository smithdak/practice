## Decisions made

- Used qualitative, evidence-backed readiness questions rather than scores or projected ROI, so the opportunity map supports selection without inventing financial outcomes.
- Defined adoption, role capability, workflow performance, output quality/safety, and operating/business outcome as separate evidence categories with distinct allowed claims.
- Made role-specific practice and changed decision rights part of enablement, rather than treating generic tool exposure as proof of readiness.
- Scaled governance controls to the actual data, access, effect, reviewability, and recovery boundary of the pilot; retained human ownership for consequential decisions.
- Treated the organizational capstone as a bounded pilot operating model and required unexecuted work to be labeled proposed.

## Risks or unresolved questions

- The module is instructional scaffolding. A pilot owner must apply applicable organizational policy, privacy, security, legal, safety, and professional requirements before execution.
- A local pilot can support only the measured claim for its stated cases and conditions; it cannot establish broad reliability, causation, organization-wide benefit, or return on investment without additional evidence.
- The curriculum's named Workflow Redesign and Agent Verification Gate Practices are planned dependencies at this point. The module links to the existing Practice index and remains usable without claiming those unpublished artifacts are available.
