# Attribution and Reuse Policy

## Outcome

Practice material can be reused commercially and adapted without asking for permission when the applicable license terms are followed. This policy makes those terms practical; the license files remain authoritative.

## Identify the license first

| Material | Default license | Reuse rule |
|---|---|---|
| Software code and scripts | [Apache License 2.0](../LICENSE-CODE) | You may use, modify, and distribute it, including commercially, subject to the Apache-2.0 terms. Keep the license, applicable notices, and notices of modified files. |
| Documentation and community content, including Practices, Guides, Labs, Stories, and templates | [CC BY 4.0](../LICENSE-CONTENT.md) | You may share and adapt it for any purpose, including commercially, with appropriate credit, a link to the license, and an indication of changes. |
| A file with its own license notice | The license stated in that file | Follow that file's notice. Do not assume the default applies. |
| Third-party material | Its original license or permission terms | Preserve the required credit and notices. Do not reuse it under a broader claim than its terms allow. |

The repository's [licensing map](../LICENSES.md) summarizes the same defaults. A license may grant broad reuse rights without granting trademark, privacy, publicity, or other rights outside that license.

## Credit for Practice content

For CC BY 4.0 material, give credit near the reused material where practical, or in an acknowledgments, credits, or source section. Include:

1. the artifact title and a link or other reasonable identifier;
2. the creator attribution supplied in the artifact, if any;
3. the source as Practice or the repository link;
4. the CC BY 4.0 license name and link; and
5. whether you changed the material.

Use this format for an unchanged excerpt or copy:

> "[Artifact title]" by Practice, [source link], licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

Use this format for an adaptation:

> Adapted from "[Artifact title]" by Practice, [source link], licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Changes: [brief description].

Replace bracketed text with the actual information. Credit must not imply that Practice or an original contributor endorses the reuse or adaptation. Do not remove a more specific attribution or source notice that appears in the artifact.

## Reusing code

For Apache-2.0 code, include a copy of [LICENSE-CODE](../LICENSE-CODE), retain applicable copyright, patent, trademark, and attribution notices, and mark modified files prominently. If your distribution includes this repository's [NOTICE](../NOTICE), retain the notices in it as required by Apache-2.0. You may add your own notices and license terms for your modifications or a combined work when they do not override the Apache-2.0 obligations for the Practice code.

Apache-2.0 does not require the CC BY content-credit format above for code. Conversely, copying a Practice article into code documentation does not turn that article into Apache-2.0 code; retain its content license and CC BY attribution unless the file says otherwise.

## Contribution certification

Practice does not require a contributor license agreement (CLA). A contributor who intentionally submits a contribution for inclusion certifies, by submitting it, that:

- they have the right to submit it and grant the applicable repository license;
- they identify copied, adapted, or third-party material and retain its required notices and attribution;
- they disclose any material license restriction or permission condition that a reviewer needs to assess; and
- unless the contribution is conspicuously marked otherwise before acceptance, it may be distributed under the default license for its type: Apache-2.0 for code and CC BY 4.0 for content.

For code, this follows the contribution submission terms in Apache-2.0. For content, the certification records the contributor's permission for Practice to publish the accepted contribution under CC BY 4.0. A contributor who cannot make that certification must not submit the material for merge. Maintainers may request clarification or decline a contribution when rights or provenance are unclear.

## Third-party and generated material review

Before submitting third-party material, record its source link, copyright or license notice, and any required attribution in the file, its metadata, or [NOTICE](../NOTICE). Prefer a summary and link over copying material that is merely publicly available. Do not submit vendor documentation, articles, transcripts, images, datasets, or other third-party material without a basis to reuse it.

Generated material receives the same review as any other contribution. The submitting Practitioner must review it for accuracy, harmful or confidential content, copied or distinctive third-party material, unsupported claims, and required source attribution before submission. A tool output is not evidence of permission, authorship, accuracy, or license compatibility. Reviewers may request source records, revisions, or removal before accepting it.

## When uncertain

Do not merge or redistribute the uncertain material. Preserve the known source information, ask the rights holder or a maintainer for direction, and use an original summary or a link if that resolves the need. This policy is an operating guide, not legal advice.
