# Moderation Model

## Outcome

Practice handles conduct concerns with human accountability while keeping ordinary quality feedback useful and low-drama. This model explains how to report, assess, decide, document, and appeal a concern without treating every weak contribution as misconduct.

This document implements the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md). It applies to community spaces and interactions named there.

## First classify the issue

| Type | Examples | Default response | Who decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quality or scope issue | Off-topic post, unclear question, duplicate discussion, unsupported claim, missing attribution, weak evidence, or an incomplete artifact. | Ask for clarification or evidence; redirect, label, close, or move the work with a visible explanation. | Contributor and human maintainer or reviewer. |
| Conduct concern | Harassment, privacy breach, threats, discrimination, retaliation, impersonation, fabricated evidence presented as real, or persistent disruption. | Preserve evidence, assess risk privately, and apply the conduct process below. | Human moderator. |
| Mixed issue | A weak contribution accompanied by hostile or retaliatory behavior. | Handle the work quality and the behavior separately; do not use a quality decision to avoid a conduct review. | Human moderator for conduct; maintainer or reviewer for quality. |

Low-signal material is not automatically a conduct violation. A person may be inexperienced, mistaken, or off topic without acting in bad faith. Conversely, technically strong work does not excuse harmful behavior.

## Roles and authority

| Role | May do | Must not do |
|---|---|---|
| Reporter or witness | Submit a private report, provide context, request privacy, and appeal a decision that affects them. | Publish sensitive evidence to obtain attention or retaliate against participants. |
| Community agent | Flag a possible quality or conduct concern; capture a link and concise, non-diagnostic summary; suggest routing or a proportionate human review. | Decide intent, investigate private information, contact parties as a moderator, remove content, restrict access, warn, suspend, ban, or resolve an appeal. |
| Human triager | Receive reports, check immediate risk, preserve evidence, request necessary context, and route the matter. | Make a final decision when they have a conflict of interest. |
| Human moderator | Investigate proportionately, decide and communicate an outcome, direct discussion, and impose or lift enforcement actions. | Silently remove people or content, or decide a case in which they have a material conflict. |
| Appeal reviewer | Review the record and appeal grounds; uphold, change, or return a decision for reconsideration. | Review their own decision or add a harsher outcome solely because an appeal was filed. |

At least one named human maintainer must own the private reporting route before public participation opens. That maintainer may designate human triagers, moderators, and appeal reviewers. When a conflict exists, the matter is routed to another eligible human; if none is available, the reporter is told that promptly and the unresolved conflict is recorded.

## Reporting and intake

1. The reporter uses the private route identified in the Code of Conduct. Public reports may be acknowledged and redirected to private intake, but sensitive details are not repeated publicly.
2. The human triager records the report date, the reporter's preferred reply method, alleged behavior, links or supplied evidence, affected spaces, requested confidentiality, immediate-risk assessment, and conflicts disclosed by decision-makers.
3. The triager confirms receipt when a reply path exists. They explain the next step without promising a result or timeline.
4. For immediate safety, privacy, or credible retaliation risk, a human moderator may temporarily pause a discussion or limit access while assessing the report. The temporary action, reason, owner, and review point are recorded. No agent may take that action.

## Evidence preservation and privacy

Keep the smallest record needed for a fair decision: original links, timestamps, relevant copies or screenshots, report notes, requests for context, conflicts, decision rationale, communications, and appeal outcome. Preserve originals where possible and identify any summaries as summaries.

Access to a report record is limited to humans who need it to triage, decide, or review the appeal. Do not publish private evidence, reporter identity, or sensitive personal information without permission unless disclosure is necessary to protect someone or required by applicable law. When a public change to content is required, use the minimum necessary action and provide a visible explanation where safe; do not silently remove content.

If evidence is unavailable, record that limitation. A decision can still address current safety or disruption, but the rationale must not claim facts that were not established.

## Assessment and decision process

1. **Triage.** Classify the matter, assess immediate risk, and check conflicts. Route quality issues to normal review and conduct concerns to a human moderator.
2. **Gather context.** Review the supplied evidence and request only information needed to assess the relevant behavior. Give the affected participant an opportunity to respond when doing so is safe and will not compromise privacy or evidence.
3. **Decide.** The human moderator records the rule or operating expectation involved, relevant evidence, uncertainty, context, impact, pattern where known, conflict handling, outcome, duration or review point, and any repair requested.
4. **Communicate.** Tell directly affected people the outcome and any expectations using the minimum necessary detail. Do not disclose protected reporter information. If a public thread or artifact needs action, explain the action visibly when safe.
5. **Follow up.** Review temporary measures at the recorded review point. Expired limits are lifted unless a human decision extends them with a new rationale.

## Enforcement guide

| Response | Use when | Required record |
|---|---|---|
| Guidance or redirection | The issue is low-signal, accidental, or resolvable through normal collaboration. | Visible explanation for a content action, if any. |
| Formal warning | A conduct expectation was crossed and a clear corrective boundary is appropriate. | Behavior, rule, expectations, and date. |
| Required pause or discussion boundary | Continued interaction risks escalation or disruption. | Scope, reason, owner, and review point. |
| Time-limited participation restriction | Serious, repeated, or unresolved harmful conduct requires a defined protection measure. | Duration, scope, rationale, and review point. |
| Access removal | The conduct, risk, or repeated evasion makes ongoing participation unsafe or unworkable. | Evidence basis, rationale, scope, appeal route, and any future review condition. |

Human moderators choose the least restrictive response that protects people and the community's ability to work. Severity, impact, intent where evidence supports it, repetition, repair, cooperation, and safety risk can affect the response. A response is not a finding about a person's worth or capability.

## Appeals

A directly affected participant may request an appeal through the private reporting route. An appeal states the decision being challenged and one or more grounds: material new evidence, a significant factual error, a process or conflict-of-interest failure, or a disproportionate outcome.

An eligible human appeal reviewer who did not make the original decision reviews the record and any new material. They may uphold the decision, change the outcome, or return it for reconsideration. The reviewer records the appeal ground, scope of review, and outcome, then communicates it with the same privacy protections as the original decision. An appeal does not automatically pause a safety measure; only a human reviewer may change it.

## Operational safeguards

- Community agents may support detection and routing, but every enforcement decision and content-removal decision is human-owned.
- Moderators do not use a public quality critique to reveal private report details.
- A contributor whose work is redirected or declined receives a reason appropriate to normal review, unless sharing more would compromise a private conduct process.
- A pattern of repeated quality problems may lead to tighter review requirements; it becomes a conduct matter only when behavior independently meets the Code of Conduct.
- Moderators periodically review records for unresolved temporary actions, conflicts, and appeals without publishing private case details.
