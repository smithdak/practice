## Summary

Created a repeatable first Workflow Clinic that turns a safe, consented
workflow discussion into a concrete draft Practice artifact—or safely declines
the case and uses a hypothetical alternative. The runbook makes intake,
selection, separate consent choices, facilitation, artifact capture, manual
follow-up, and human publication review operational.

## Decisions made

- Made participation, note capture, attribution, recording, and publication
  separate opt-in choices; declining publication has no adverse consequence.
- Set the default durable output to a `proposed` Practice draft with no claimed
  evidence, while allowing a Note or no artifact when the material cannot be
  honestly generalized.
- Kept all final selection, publication, moderation, and consequential-workflow
  decisions human-owned; the Steward, Librarian, and Research Auditor have
  bounded supporting roles.
- Required a hypothetical substitute or safe decline whenever a workflow is
  sensitive, unsuitable, unreviewable, or not consented for capture.

## Risks or unresolved questions

- The runbook intentionally does not establish a recording, retention, or
  private-intake system; no such system is required to run the clinic safely.
- The publication reviewer and human escalation route must be named before a
  real participant artifact can enter review. The runbook does not invent an
  owner or a platform capability.

## Deferred opportunities

- Create separately owned reusable intake, consent, redaction, and
  session-record templates after a human decides the approved private
  retention process.
- Test the runbook against hypothetical workflows from individual, operator,
  and organizational contexts, then revise only observed ambiguities.
