## Summary

Phase 4 builds the A3 substrate and promotes nothing. X6 is the governance half:
the record a human signs, and the evidence they would weigh before signing.

Two files were written under `ops/autonomy/`.

`PROMOTION_PROPOSAL.md` is the fillable proposal for promoting one operation to
A3. It opens with the risk it closes — not that promotion becomes too easy, but
that it happens informally with no record of what was weighed — and states twice
that filling it in promotes nothing. It carries an admissibility section (the
operation must be catalogued, off the permanently ineligible list, already
holding R1 to R3, backed by a dossier, with an executed reversal and an observed
guard refusal), then eleven fillable sections: identity; what unattended running
is actually worth and who has agreed to read the output; write scope and why it
is the narrowest that works, including a negative check and a warning that the
guard's `fnmatch` globs and `ops/cadence.yaml`'s `Path.glob` globs mean different
things; the reversal recorded as an executed event with a date, a ledger entry,
and the role that performed it; blast radius after a month unnoticed; the
inherited demotion triggers split into machine-observable and human-observable;
evidence that exists and evidence that does not; pull-request review ownership
and rhythm; the review point, with silence explicitly not renewal; a prose
"Reasons to decline" section with six prompts and a minimum of three specific
reasons; and an unsigned signature block. It then states that signing is half —
a human transcribes the entry into `ops/autonomy/promotions.yaml` field by field,
and the kill switch is a separate, independent release — and closes with what the
record cannot do.

`CANDIDATES.md` holds one dossier per catalogued operation, each with the same
eight sections: what it would do unattended, write scope, reversal, blast radius,
evidence that exists, evidence that does not, the specific way it goes wrong, and
the decision a human would face with what they would need first. Every claim was
read from the implementing script rather than inferred, and several were verified
by running the scripts. No dossier recommends promotion; two say plainly that the
operation should stay where it is.

## Decisions made

- **Fill-in convention.** Followed `templates/RELEASE_EVIDENCE.md`: every
  placeholder is an inline code span such as `<role>` or `<YYYY-MM-DD>`, with
  italic guidance beneath saying what to record and what to leave out. No
  bracketed token appears in visible prose, so release validation accepts both
  files.
- **"Reasons to decline" is prose, never boxes.** The section asks for at least
  three specific reasons and answers to six prompts. A checkbox would record that
  risk was considered; the prose records what was considered.
- **The dossiers were rewritten against X2's catalog once it landed.** The first
  draft assumed the phase plan's example scopes. The committed catalog differs in
  four of five entries: `metrics-snapshot` shares `ops/status/*.md` with
  `cadence-snapshot`; `contract-drift-check` runs only
  `scripts/validate_agents.py` and has an empty write scope; `staleness-sweep`
  runs `scripts/check_links.py` and has an empty write scope; and
  `release-brief-draft` uses a date-shaped glob under `release/briefs/`. The
  dossiers now quote the catalog and say the catalog is authoritative.
- **No dossier recommends promotion, and two decline plainly.**
  `contract-drift-check` and `release-brief-draft` are stated as unsuitable as
  they stand, with the reason in each case. The other three end with the decision
  a human faces and the specific evidence that is missing.
- **Numbers were made robust where they move.** Counts that change as workers add
  files (markdown file totals, as-of coverage) carry their measurement date and a
  note to re-measure. Per-file test counts were kept; whole-suite totals were
  dropped, since the suite grew from 562 to 688 during this wave.

## Risks or unresolved questions

- **`make checks` does not pass at the time of this handoff**, for two failures
  in files X6 does not own: `scripts/autonomy_guard.py` and `scripts/ledger.py`
  are not wired into `.github/workflows/ci.yml` and are not in
  `ValidatorWiringTests.EXEMPT`. W2 owns both paths. Nothing in X6's files
  depends on the fix.
- **Two glob dialects for what looks like one pattern.** The guard matches a
  write scope with `fnmatchcase`, where `*` crosses a slash; `ops/cadence.yaml`
  evidence globs are matched with `Path.glob`, where it does not. So
  `ops/status/*.md` in the catalog also permits `ops/status/archive/old.md`,
  while `ops/*.md` in the cadence index means only the eight files directly under
  `ops/`. Both documents warn about this, but the divergence is real and a future
  reader will meet it again.
- **`cadence-snapshot` and `metrics-snapshot` share `ops/status/*.md`.** Two
  operations writing into one directory produce an interleaved series where a gap
  in one is masked by the presence of the other. Recorded as a finding in the
  `metrics-snapshot` dossier; the resolution is X2's or W2's, not X6's.
- **`staleness-sweep` overlaps `cadence-snapshot`.** The cadence report already
  computes the staleness half of the sweep, through the same functions in
  `scripts/check_links.py`. Promoting both would put two unattended runs on one
  fact. Recorded, not resolved.
- **`scripts/demotion_check.py` is referenced before it exists.** Section 6 of the
  proposal names it as the detector for the observable triggers. It is X4's owned
  output in wave X-2 and was not present when this handoff was written. If X4
  lands under a different name, the reference needs one edit.
- **Neither file has been read by anyone who was not in this task.** The proposal
  is written for a signer who arrives cold; that assumption is untested.
