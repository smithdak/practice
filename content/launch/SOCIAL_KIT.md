# Practice social launch kit

Reusable launch copy for **Practice — the open community for AI practitioners.**
This kit keeps one claim set across channels while adapting length, pacing, and
format. Replace every bracketed placeholder before publishing.

## Message spine

Use these points in this order when space allows:

1. A plausible AI answer is not the same as work you can accept or ship.
2. Practice is an open community for people making AI useful in real contexts.
3. It shares inspectable methods and artifacts for context, workflow design,
   verification, and responsible human review.
4. Git holds durable, versioned work; Buzz is the place for humans and agents
   to coordinate around it.
5. Start with one non-sensitive task: inspect one canonical Practice and share
   what happened.

The exact wording can change by channel; these claims must not be upgraded into
claims of traction, reliability, safety, or outcomes that have not been
documented.

## Response templates

Use a specific reply when possible. Replace placeholders; do not imply that a
reply is a testimonial, measured result, or endorsement.

### “Where do I start?”

Start with one bounded, non-sensitive task you already understand. Read the
relevant canonical Practice, note its inputs and checks, and share your first
action in [#START_HERE_CHANNEL]: [START_HERE_URL]

### “Is this a prompt library?”

No. Prompts can be part of an implementation, but Practice focuses on the
surrounding method: context, boundaries, permissions, evaluation, review,
recovery, and ownership. The goal is work another person can inspect and reuse.

### “Which model or tool should I use?”

Practice is model- and platform-agnostic. Choose tools that fit your task and
constraints; keep the durable method clear enough to compare or replace the
tool. Share the implementation details and limitations in [DISCUSSION_URL].

### “Can an agent do this for me?”

An agent may help gather approved context, draft, test, or surface gaps. Give it
only the access the task needs, keep its output reviewable, and retain human
ownership of consequential decisions. See [VERIFICATION_PRACTICE_URL].

### “What should I share?”

Share the task boundary, safe inputs, method, checks, result, and limitations.
Remove secrets, personal information, confidential material, and unsafe
operating details. If the work is not safe to publish, share the reusable
pattern without the sensitive data.

### “Is this a claim that the workflow is reliable?”

No. A method should earn confidence through its stated checks, evidence, and
reproductions. Practice asks contributors to record uncertainty and failure
modes instead of treating fluent output or a passing check as proof of
everything.

### “I found a problem.”

Thank you for making it inspectable. Please include the relevant artifact or
line, the steps or conditions that reproduce the issue, and a proposed
correction if you have one: [ISSUE_URL]

### “How can I contribute?”

Try a canonical Practice on a non-sensitive task, then contribute a correction,
variation, reproduction, Lab, Story, or Note. Begin with the contribution
guidance at [CONTRIBUTING_URL].
