# Organizational AI: change how work operates, not merely which tool is available

An organization has not transformed work because people opened an AI tool, attended a session, or reported that they liked an output. Change begins with a business or service outcome, makes the affected work and decisions visible, and tests a bounded new way of operating under accountable human ownership.

This is Module 6 of [The AI-Native Practitioner](README.md). It builds on the workflow evidence from [Module 4](04-automation-agents.md) and an available system or implementation brief. It is model-, provider-, and framework-agnostic. The proposed [Workflow Redesign](../../practices/002-workflow-redesign.md) and [Verification Gate](../../practices/003-verification-gate.md) methods are available for trial; this module preserves their evidence limits.

## What you will produce

For one team or organizational workflow, produce:

1. A work inventory and **opportunity map** that connects a candidate intervention to an operating outcome, current evidence, consequence, and test boundary.
2. A change brief with the before and proposed workflow, role and decision-right changes, knowledge architecture, and human accountability.
3. A role-specific enablement plan and a governance design proportional to the pilot's consequence.
4. A measurement record that separates baseline, adoption, demonstrated capability, workflow performance, and business outcome.
5. An organizational capstone: a bounded pilot operating model, or a clearly labeled proposal when execution is not authorized.

The module is complete when a reviewer can see what work would change, who decides, what evidence would justify a next decision, and how the pilot stops or is contained. It does not establish a return on investment, a universal capability, production readiness, or permission to expand the boundary.

## Start with work and its operating outcome

Do not begin with a tool inventory or a request to “find AI use cases.” Start where work is repeated, delayed, error-prone, difficult to hand off, or materially affects an outcome that a team already cares about. An **operating outcome** is a result the workflow is meant to produce, such as a complete reviewed case, an accurate handoff, a resolved request, or a decision made with required evidence. It is not “more AI use.”

Describe the current method before proposing a future one. Include the constraints that make a seemingly simple intervention unsuitable: approval, source quality, access, service level, exceptions, downstream dependencies, and the consequence of a wrong result. Use safe descriptions or a representative synthetic case if real details are not authorized for this record.

| Workflow and operating outcome | Trigger, volume or cadence if known | Current roles, decisions, and handoffs | Current evidence or baseline | Constraint, failure consequence, or affected people | Candidate question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  | observed / unknown |  | What bounded uncertainty or drafting burden might assistance reduce? |

State unknowns plainly. For example, an existing complaint about slow work is not a measured baseline; record it as a concern until a consistent local measure exists. Do not estimate savings, revenue, headcount reduction, accuracy, or return on investment from the existence of a candidate workflow.

**Checkable outcome:** an accountable work owner can recognize the current workflow, its intended operating outcome, and at least one consequence that constrains a change.

## Make an opportunity map, not an ROI spreadsheet

An **opportunity map** compares candidate changes against the conditions required to test them safely and usefully. It is not a prediction of financial return. A candidate can be important and still be the wrong first pilot because its inputs are unavailable, its output cannot be reviewed, or its failure cannot be contained.

For each candidate, record evidence or `unknown` for the following decision questions:

| Decision question | What to inspect | A reason to defer, narrow, or keep human-owned |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome relevance | Which operating outcome, service obligation, or decision would this affect? | The outcome is vague or no work owner recognizes it. |
| Work definition | Are the trigger, inputs, steps, exceptions, output, and handoffs visible? | The proposed task is a label for many different kinds of work. |
| Reviewability | Can a qualified person inspect the proposed output before a consequential effect? | Correctness, appropriateness, or source support cannot be checked in time. |
| Knowledge and access readiness | Are authoritative sources, minimum access, freshness rules, and conflict ownership known? | Required information is unauthorized, stale, conflicting, or has no owner. |
| Reversibility and consequence | What happens if the result is wrong, late, missing, or misused; can it be stopped or corrected? | A material effect cannot be contained with the proposed controls. |
| Role readiness | Do affected roles have a named owner, available reviewer, and a feasible changed task? | The design shifts hidden work or accountability onto people without agreement. |
| Evidence feasibility | Can the team preserve a comparable baseline and inspect process and output evidence? | The intended claim cannot be measured or even bounded locally. |

### Exercise 2 — Build the opportunity map

Use a short evidence statement for each cell rather than a fabricated score. A simple `ready`, `not ready`, or `unknown` is useful only when accompanied by the reason and owner of the next decision.

| Candidate workflow | Operating outcome | Proposed intervention and non-goal | Reviewability and consequence | Knowledge/access readiness | Baseline/evidence available | Recommendation and reason | Owner and next decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  | test / narrow / defer / do not use AI |  |

Select only one candidate for this module. Prefer the smallest change that could answer a real operating question. A draft placed in an existing review queue may be a better first pilot than a workflow that sends, approves, purchases, changes access, or makes a decision.

**Checkable outcome:** the selected opportunity has a named operating outcome, visible boundary, known or explicitly unknown evidence, and a credible reason it is safer or more informative than the alternatives.

## Redesign the workflow, roles, and decision rights together

Adding a drafting step can move work rather than remove it. A reviewer may receive more items, a subject-matter expert may need to maintain source material, or an operator may need to resolve new exceptions. Make these changes visible before a pilot.

Keep consequential decisions human-owned unless the accountable organization has separately authorized a narrower boundary. An AI-generated recommendation, usage by many people, or a system's ability to execute a step does not transfer accountability.

For every role, answer three questions:

1. What work is removed, added, or changed?
2. What may this role decide, approve, correct, or escalate—and what remains outside its authority?
3. What evidence does the role need to do that work and show that it happened?

Do not call a role “responsible for AI” when it only receives an output. Name the actual work: source steward, operator, reviewer, decision owner, incident contact, or service owner. One person may hold several roles in a small pilot, but the decision rights should still be distinct.

**Checkable outcome:** a person affected by the pilot can tell whether their work, authority, queue, or escalation obligation changes and can identify the human owner of each consequential decision.

## Treat knowledge as an operating asset

A human-reviewed workflow that uses model output often exposes knowledge that was implicit: which source controls, how an exception is interpreted, where a definition lives, and who may update it. A **knowledge architecture** is the practical arrangement of those sources, rules, ownership, access boundaries, and maintenance decisions. It is not a large repository by default.

Begin with the knowledge needed for the selected workflow only. Separate authoritative sources from examples, temporary case inputs, generated drafts, and reviewer decisions. More material is not automatically better; irrelevant, stale, conflicting, or unauthorized material makes review harder.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Include the source or rule a reviewer uses to check the output. If the workflow retains case state, drafts, feedback, or run records, state why, who can access it, how it is corrected, and when it is reviewed or discarded. Do not use a memory store, prompt, or agent handoff to bypass ordinary access controls.

**Checkable outcome:** an authorized reviewer can identify what controls the work, who maintains it, what is excluded, and what happens when the knowledge is stale or disputed.

## Enable roles to perform the changed work

**Adoption** is evidence that a person or team encountered, accessed, or used a new method. **Capability** is evidence that a role can perform its changed work to a stated standard under the actual workflow conditions. Attendance, logins, prompts sent, and favorable reactions can be adoption signals; none by itself demonstrates capability or operating improvement.

| Record type | Question it can answer | Examples of evidence | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enablement activity | Was role-specific preparation offered or completed? | Briefing attendance; completed walkthrough; access confirmation. | That the role can perform the work correctly. |
| Adoption signal | Did a role begin trying the approved method? | Eligible cases entered; drafts submitted for review; active use in the bounded pilot. | Quality, safety, speed, or business impact. |
| Capability evidence | Can the role complete its changed task under the stated check? | Reviewed practice case; correct escalation; source selection and output check performed against a rubric. | That every future case or team will perform the same way. |
| Workflow performance | Did the bounded process operate as designed? | Required fields present; review queue status; correction count; exception path followed. | A causal business result without a relevant comparison. |
| Business outcome | Did the operating outcome change under a stated local measure? | Comparable service, quality, cost, risk, or outcome measure with method and limitations. | General ROI, lasting impact, or causation beyond the evidence. |

Design enablement around the work each role must now perform. A generic product demonstration may be useful orientation, but it is not sufficient preparation for a reviewer, source steward, operator, or incident contact.

## Apply governance proportional to consequence

Governance is the allocation of decision rights, controls, evidence, and responses that lets people operate and change a workflow responsibly. It should fit the consequence and uncertainty of the specific pilot. It is neither an excuse to skip controls for a seemingly simple draft nor a requirement to build a heavyweight approval program before a low-consequence, review-only test.

Assess the particular boundary: data sensitivity, access and action permissions, effect on people or commitments, reversibility, output checkability, and the ability to detect and contain failure. When any factor is unknown, narrow or pause the pilot rather than presume a low risk.

| Pilot condition | Minimum governance questions | Examples of proportionate controls |
|---|---|---|
| Review-only draft using authorized, low-consequence inputs | Who owns the task and reviews the output? What data is allowed? How is a bad draft prevented from becoming an effect? | Named reviewer; approved source list; draft-only output; no send/publish/change permission; correction and stop rule. |
| Internal workflow with controlled data or operational dependency | Who grants access? Which source controls? How are exceptions, failures, and changes observed? | Least-privilege access; source and version register; run or queue record; exception owner; change review; retention rule. |
| Workflow affecting people, money, access, commitments, safety, regulated obligations, or public claims | Who has authority for the effect? Can an independent or qualified reviewer check it? How is impact contained and reported? | Explicit human approval; stronger validation or independent review where warranted; incident route; effect log; rollback or compensating action; pause authority. |

These examples do not replace applicable organizational, legal, privacy, security, safety, or professional requirements. Escalate when the accountable owner cannot determine authorization, handling, review, or recovery conditions.

## Measure operating change without collapsing the evidence

Before the pilot, state what is already known and how each proposed measure will be collected. A baseline may be an approved prior artifact, a manual run under comparable conditions, an existing service record, or `unknown`. Do not retrofit a baseline after a favorable result.

Choose a small set of measures that directly informs the next decision. Counts can be useful, but count only what the team can define and preserve consistently. A change in the number of AI interactions is usually an adoption signal, not evidence of quality, safety, or value.

### Exercise 7 — Build the measurement ledger

| Evidence category | Decision question | Baseline or current state | Method, case boundary, and owner | Pilot observation field | Limitation and allowed claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adoption | Are eligible roles using the approved pilot method? |  |  |  | Adoption only; not capability or impact. |
| Capability | Can each role perform its changed task to the stated check? |  |  |  | Evidence applies to the observed task and conditions. |
| Workflow performance | Does the process meet its defined completeness, review, or exception rule? |  |  |  | Does not prove business impact by itself. |
| Output quality or safety | Does the output meet its task-specific verification rule? |  |  |  | Do not infer general reliability. |
| Operating or business outcome | Did the selected local outcome change under a comparable measure? |  |  |  | State confounders and do not convert one pilot into ROI. |

For a time measure, define start and finish boundaries consistently and include review, source checking, rework, and exception handling if they belong to the workflow. For quality, use a task-specific rubric or verification record rather than a favorable impression. For risk, define the condition, detection method, and observed event record; “no incident reported” does not prove absence of risk.

After each review, use the weakest accurate description:

- **Proposal:** the intervention and measurement plan are designed but not executed.
- **Observed process change:** the documented workflow or role behavior changed in the stated pilot cases.
- **Measured result:** a stated measure changed under the recorded conditions and limitations.
- **Hypothesis:** a plausible but untested explanation or expected effect.

**Checkable outcome:** a reviewer can point to different records for activity, role capability, workflow behavior, and operating outcome, and can see which claims are not yet supported.

## Organizational capstone — Bounded pilot operating model

Create a pilot operating model for the selected opportunity. If you lack authorization to run it, label every unobserved field **proposed** and do not imply a result. The capstone can begin with review-only work; it does not require organization-wide rollout, unattended execution, or a claim of return on investment.

```text
Pilot name and status: proposed / active / paused / completed / ended
Operating outcome and current baseline or explicit unknown:
Selected workflow, case scope, duration or review window if locally set, and non-goals:
Before workflow and proposed workflow, including handoffs and exceptions:
Affected roles, changed tasks, decision rights, and accountable owners:
Knowledge sources, authority, access, maintenance owner, and conflict rule:
Intervention: deterministic, AI-assisted, or bounded agentic step; reason for this boundary:
Role-specific enablement, practice evidence, and support path:
Governance: data/access limits, approval, review, monitoring, incident route, and change control:
Baseline and measurement ledger; records to retain and who checks them:
Review cadence, participants, and decision record location:
Stop conditions, pause authority, rollback/containment, and any resumption rule:
Decision rule: retain, revise, narrow, expand, pause, or end; evidence required for each:
Observed results, if any, with case scope, method, limitations, and remaining uncertainty:
```

At the review point, record a decision rather than a narrative of enthusiasm. Expansion requires evidence that the defined boundary operated acceptably, affected roles can perform their changed work, controls are adequate for the proposed new consequence, and the next measurement question is explicit. A successful demonstration, high usage, or a polished output alone is insufficient.

### Capstone evidence checklist

- The selected opportunity traces to a real operating outcome and a named owner.
- The before and proposed workflows show every changed role, decision right, handoff, and exception.
- The map explains why this candidate is testable without inventing ROI.
- Knowledge sources, maintenance, access, and conflict handling are explicit.
- Enablement tests role-specific work; adoption is recorded separately from capability.
- Governance, approval, monitoring, incident response, and recovery are proportionate to consequence.
- Baseline, process, output, capability, and operating-outcome evidence are not conflated.
- The pilot has non-goals, stop conditions, a decision owner, and a revise/expand/pause/end rule.
- Any observed claim names its method, scope, and limitation; unrun work is labeled proposed.

The capstone is acceptable when an authorized reviewer can decide whether the bounded pilot should proceed, change, pause, or end without assuming unmeasured benefits or transferring consequential judgment to a tool.

## Route extensions

**Applied:** document adjacent roles, workflow ownership, source maintenance, review workload, and the evidence needed to retain or revise the changed method.

**Technical:** add service ownership, implementation change control, operational records, incident handling, decommissioning, and the relationship between system boundaries and organizational decision rights.

**Organizational:** prepare or run the bounded pilot with the accountable leaders and affected roles. Preserve the decision record and evaluate operating change only against the predeclared measures and conditions.

## Failure modes to catch early

| Failure mode | What it looks like | Correct response |
|---|---|---|
| Tool-first selection | The candidate is chosen because a product is available, while the operating outcome and owner are unclear. | Return to the work inventory and select or define a bounded outcome. |
| Activity claimed as impact | Attendance, access, prompt count, or favorable feedback is presented as transformation. | Record it as enablement or adoption only; add capability and workflow evidence. |
| Invisible transferred work | Review, source maintenance, exception handling, or queue burden appears after launch. | Map the role change, assign owner and capacity decision, then revise or narrow. |
| Governance mismatch | A consequential workflow has draft-level controls, or a review-only pilot is blocked by undefined blanket process. | Reassess the actual data, access, effect, checkability, and recovery boundary. |
| Unowned knowledge | Sources conflict or become stale and no one is responsible for resolving them. | Name source authority and maintenance owner; stop affected cases until resolved. |
| Baseline after the fact | A result is declared better without a comparable current method or stated measurement boundary. | Label the observation limited or proposed; establish the baseline before the next test. |
| Premature expansion | One promising run is used to widen access, autonomy, or consequence. | Keep the boundary, inspect evidence and failures, and require a new governance and measurement decision. |
