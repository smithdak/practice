# Practice Founding Brief

## The idea

AI capability is advancing faster than most people and organizations can absorb it. Access is no longer the primary problem. The harder problem is turning rapidly changing tools into reliable capability.

Practice exists to answer, in the open:

- How should I work differently?
- What should I automate?
- How do I build reliable agents and workflows?
- How should a team adopt AI without producing chaos?
- How do we measure whether a change actually helped?
- How do we keep improving as the technology changes?

Practice is an open-source community for people putting AI to work. We learn, experiment, build, implement, measure, document, and give the useful result back to the commons.

## Mission

Make practical AI capability open and accessible to everyone.

The objective is not literacy alone. A Practitioner should become able to do something useful they could not do before.

## North star

Build the open standard for becoming AI-native.

## What Practice is

Practice sits at the intersection of education, open source, community, engineering, and organizational transformation. It is:

- a learning and implementation community;
- a public body of operational knowledge;
- an experimentation network;
- an open-source ecosystem;
- a professional practice that improves through evidence.

## What Practice is not

Practice is not an AI news feed, prompt directory, affiliate catalog, model-release hype cycle, or passive discussion group.

The default progression is:

> Learn → Apply → Measure → Share → Improve

## The Practice principle

> Learn it. Build it. Use it. Share it.

- **Learn it:** understand enough to use the technology deliberately.
- **Build it:** create a workflow, skill, agent, tool, system, experiment, or process.
- **Use it:** put the thing into real work; reality is the benchmark.
- **Share it:** document successes and failures so the next Practitioner starts further ahead.

## Practitioners

A Practitioner is someone actively developing the ability to put AI to useful work. The identity is not limited by job title or technical depth.

## Capability ladder

1. Learn
2. Use
3. Automate
4. Build
5. Transform

The ladder maps capabilities, not status. A person may be advanced in one domain and new in another.

## Open Practices

A mature Practice states its problem, context, method, implementation, tools, example, evaluation, tradeoffs, evidence, and variations. It is versioned and improved as evidence changes.

## Practice Guides

Guides combine Practices into opinionated implementation paths. The first flagship Guide is **The AI-Native Practitioner**.

## Projects, Labs, Notes, and Stories

- Projects produce usable open-source software.
- Labs run reproducible experiments.
- Notes preserve smaller observations.
- Stories document real before-and-after implementations without inventing evidence.

## Contribution

Useful contributions include corrections, failure reports, examples, implementations, experiments, code, translations, maintenance, and helping another Practitioner.

The highest-status behavior is making it easier for the next person.

## Open-source philosophy

Knowledge, Practices, Guides, reference implementations, and experiments should default to openness. Commercial reuse is a success condition when attribution and licensing terms are respected.

## Long-term direction

Practice may eventually support enterprise transformation, implementation, hosted tooling, events, practitioner programs, and other commercial layers. Those emerge from demonstrated community needs and do not restrict the open core.
