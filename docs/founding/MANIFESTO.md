# The Practice Manifesto

## The hard part is making AI work

AI can produce an answer in seconds. That does not mean the answer is correct, the workflow is reliable, or the work has improved.

The implementation gap is where the real difficulty begins. A useful demonstration must survive contact with incomplete context, changing tools, security constraints, review standards, edge cases, and the people responsible for the outcome. A promising experiment must become a repeatable way of working. A team must know what to delegate, what to verify, and who remains accountable. Without that discipline, speed creates more output but not necessarily more capability.

Practice exists to close that gap in the open.

We are a community for people putting AI to useful work: people learning their first dependable workflow, engineers building agents, operators redesigning recurring processes, leaders changing how teams work, and everyone between. We call a community member a Practitioner because this is an active identity. It comes from doing the work, examining the result, and helping others do it better—not from a title, credential, or preferred tool.

Our mission is to make practical AI capability open and accessible to everyone. Our north star is to build the open standard for becoming AI-native: not allegiance to a vendor, but the ability to choose, use, evaluate, and improve AI systems deliberately.

## Capability is built through practice

Information matters, but knowing about a capability is different from having it. Capability grows when knowledge is applied to real work and the result is examined.

Our progression is simple:

> Learn → Apply → Measure → Share → Improve

Learn enough to act deliberately. Apply what you learned to a real problem. Measure what changed, including what failed. Share the method and evidence. Improve it with what others discover.

This cycle can begin at any level. A Practitioner might learn how context changes an answer, use AI in a daily task, automate a repeatable workflow, build an agent or tool, or transform how a team operates. These stages—Learn, Use, Automate, Build, and Transform—map capabilities, not status. Someone may be advanced in one domain and a beginner in another. That is normal. Practice rewards honest starting points and demonstrated progress.

Our ethos names the commitment:

> **Learn it. Build it. Use it. Share it.**

Learn it so that choices are informed rather than accidental. Build it so that an idea becomes something another person can inspect. Use it because reality reveals what a demonstration hides. Share it so that the next Practitioner begins with a tested starting point instead of repeating the same discovery alone.

## A Practice is more than a tip

We use **Practice** to mean a reusable, tested method. A mature Practice explains the problem it addresses, the context in which it applies, the steps and tools involved, the expected output, how to evaluate that output, the tradeoffs and failure modes, and the evidence available so far. It also records meaningful variations, because methods should adapt without becoming vague.

This standard is intentionally higher than a prompt pasted without context or a confident claim without a test. A prompt can be part of a Practice, but it is not the whole method. The surrounding decisions—inputs, permissions, review, evaluation, and recovery when something goes wrong—often determine whether the work is dependable.

Different artifacts serve different stages of knowledge. A Note preserves an observation worth developing. A Lab makes an experiment reproducible. A Story documents a real implementation, including its before state, intervention, result, and lessons. A Project produces usable open-source software or infrastructure. A Guide combines multiple Practices into an opinionated path toward an outcome. Each artifact should say what is known, what is proposed, and what remains uncertain.

This is how a body of operational knowledge becomes useful: not by pretending every contribution is final, but by making each one clear enough to test and improve.

## Evidence is part of the work

AI systems can sound persuasive when they are wrong. People can remember wins and overlook quiet failures. Teams can confuse faster output with better outcomes. Evidence is how we resist those habits.

We do not require every contribution to arrive with a formal study. We do require honesty about what supports it. An untested idea is a proposal. A worked example is an example, not proof of a universal result. A personal observation is useful when its context and limits are visible. A measured implementation should explain what was measured and how. A failure report can be more valuable than a polished success because it narrows the conditions under which a method should be trusted.

Evaluation must fit the consequence. Low-risk drafting may need a quick human review. A workflow that influences customers, money, access, safety, or consequential decisions demands stronger checks, explicit ownership, and a way to stop or recover. Reliability is not a property we declare once. It is maintained through tests, review, monitoring, and revision as tools and contexts change.

Practice is model-, platform-, and framework-agnostic for the same reason. Tools change. Useful knowledge should distinguish durable method from temporary interface. Tool-specific implementations are welcome when they reveal enough context for someone to reproduce, compare, or replace them.

## Open is how the work compounds

Practical knowledge often stays trapped in private chats, closed teams, conference notes, and one person's memory. When the lesson disappears, everyone downstream pays the discovery cost again.

Practice defaults to openness. Reusable knowledge, experiments, reference implementations, and code should be available to inspect, version, fork, adapt, and improve. Public artifacts create a durable record of what changed and why. They let disagreement become a proposed revision. They let a local solution become a shared starting point.

Openness is not permission to expose what should remain protected. Secrets, personal information, client-confidential material, and unsafe operational details do not belong in public contributions. Good documentation abstracts or removes sensitive context while preserving the method. Responsible openness makes knowledge reusable without making people or systems vulnerable.

Commercial use of open work is not a betrayal of this project. When licensing and attribution are respected, use in a product, service, or organization is evidence that the commons is useful. The open core remains available for the next person to learn from and extend.

## Humans and agents work together

AI agents can help a community gather context, connect related work, draft improvements, test artifacts, and surface questions. Humans contribute judgment, lived context, accountability, care, and the authority to make consequential decisions. Practice is designed for both to participate, with clear roles rather than blurred responsibility.

An agent's contribution should be reviewable. Its access should be limited to what its work requires. Its recommendations should be distinguishable from human decisions. Agents may help triage or recommend moderation actions; they do not silently remove people or content. Humans remain responsible for community governance and for accepting work that carries real-world consequences.

The point is neither to keep agents at the edge nor to hand them the wheel. It is to develop better patterns for collaboration: context that can be shared safely, tasks with explicit boundaries, outputs that can be inspected, and checkpoints where human judgment matters. Those patterns should themselves become Practices, open to examination and improvement.

## Contribution is the measure of belonging

Practice is not a stage with a small group of experts and a large audience. It is a workshop. Participation means moving useful knowledge forward.

A contribution can be a new method, but it does not have to be. Correct an unclear step. Report the case where a workflow broke. Add an implementation in another tool. Reproduce a Lab and compare the result. Translate a Guide. Improve an evaluation checklist. Maintain code. Ask a precise question that reveals missing context. Help another Practitioner turn an isolated result into something reusable.

We value specificity over certainty and usefulness over performance. We expect disagreement, because contexts differ and evidence changes. The productive response is to show the conditions, propose a revision, and make the alternative testable.

The highest-status action in this community is simple:

> I learned something useful and made it easier for the next person.

## Begin with one piece of real work

Choose a recurring task you know well. Write down its inputs, its current steps, and what a good result looks like. Identify one bounded place where AI might help. Build the smallest workable change. Keep a human review point. Try it on real examples you are allowed to use. Record what improved, what did not, and where it failed.

Then share the result at the maturity it has earned: as a Note if it is an observation, a Lab if others can reproduce the test, a Practice if the method is ready to reuse, a Story if you can document a real implementation, or a Project if you built working software.

Do not wait for a grand breakthrough. Bring one honest lesson, one tested method, one useful correction, or one well-described failure. Help us turn scattered experiments into shared capability.

Learn it. Build it. Use it. Share it. Then make it easier for the next Practitioner to do the same.
