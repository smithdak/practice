# Quality Bar

Every merged artifact must satisfy the following.

## Useful

A reader can take a concrete next action. Avoid motivational filler, generic AI claims, and lists without operational guidance.

## Reproducible

Practices and Labs state inputs, steps, outputs, evaluation, failure modes, and variations. Examples never pretend to be measured case studies unless evidence exists.

## Honest

Do not fabricate product capabilities, metrics, citations, quotations, users, outcomes, or community activity. Clearly label proposals, hypotheses, examples, and unverified assumptions.

## Current where it matters

Current technical claims use primary sources and include an as-of date. Prefer timeless principles when a changing product detail is unnecessary.

## Model-agnostic

Do not make a single vendor the center of Practice. Tool-specific examples are allowed when they are clearly examples rather than requirements.

## Concise but complete

Use direct language, descriptive headings, examples, and checklists. Remove repeated framing and empty superlatives.

## Accessible to mixed skill levels

Define specialized terms on first use. Preserve technical rigor without assuming every Practitioner is an engineer.

## Safe to publish

No secrets, private keys, access tokens, client-confidential details, personal information, or unlicensed third-party content.

## Reviewable

Every task changes only its owned files, commits the result, runs validation, and writes a handoff that names unresolved risks.
